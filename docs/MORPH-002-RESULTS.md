# MORPH-002: matched event order and persistence results

Status: completed exploratory mechanism assay. The [protocol](protocols/MORPH-002-matched-event-order.md) was committed locally before executing the assay. Its SHA-256 and the executed assay-source/genome hashes are embedded in the [readable summary](../results/MORPH-002-summary.json) and [complete losslessly compressed record](../results/MORPH-002-matched-event-order.json.gz). This was not an externally preregistered confirmatory study. No threshold, tax, dormant-age, or core organ logic was changed after outcomes were inspected.

## What was held constant

Thirty developmental turns contain the same complete event objects, same eight record inputs when applicable, same total pressure of 2.8 synthetic units, same absolute time schedule, and same credited resource opportunities. The two complete friction events occur at turns 1/2 in EARLY and swap with neutral events at turns 17/18 in LATE. Identical histories are rerun exactly, not counted as independent stochastic replications.

Each developmental comparison begins from byte-identical starting states. All matching and deterministic-replay gates passed. Eight equal-age recall questions are evaluated on independent copies without mutating the source agent. Receipt dates and engineer hashes are excluded from the content-state metric. Captures occur at the developmental endpoint and after 8, 32, and 64 turns of identical continuation.

## Transient structural result

In the pruning-only developmental case, EARLY memory grows at turn 2 and atrophies at turn 18. LATE memory grows at turn 18 and remains mounted at the turn-30 endpoint. The endpoint topologies differ, but both score zero of eight recall questions because neither acquired records. After eight neutral continuation turns, both topologies and content states agree. They remain equal at the later captures.

This is a real order-dependent endpoint topology with no measured functional advantage and no persistence through the shared neutral continuation. It should not be described as irreversible architectural divergence. The preallocated-memory control also shows a transient endpoint topology difference because it can prune and regrow. That is further reason not to attribute this structural effect uniquely to an initially empty zygote.

## Persistent acquisition result

In the retained-memory developmental case, records arrive at turns 3 through 10. EARLY has already grown its memory organ and retains all eight. LATE has not yet grown that organ and cannot recover those vanished inputs. Common anchor queries at turns 14 and 24 keep attempted use explicit without supplying any missing answer. At the endpoint, both arms have the same organ set, while exact source recall is eight of eight in EARLY and zero of eight in LATE.

That eight-record difference persists at all four captures, including 64 further identical continuation turns. The continuation queries the anchor every eighth turn, so this is persistence under continued attempted use. It is not retention through total inactivity or unlimited durability. The memory stores have different content; the post-development topologies are equal.

Deleting the memory organ on evaluation copies makes both arms score zero, eliminating the recall gap. The intervention removes both stored information and the structure that reads it; it establishes their joint causal necessity here, not a uniquely identified weight, schema feature, or metacognitive mechanism.

## Control results and control-label correction

The growth-disabled condition never mounts an organ and scores zero in both orders at every capture. It rules out a hidden kernel episodic store on these exact-key questions. In the retained-memory preallocation control, both arms store the same eight records from the start and score eight of eight; its order gap is zero throughout. The positive developmental acquisition gap therefore depends on the growth-timing boundary in this favorable setup.

The original protocol calls the preallocated condition fixed-memory/fixed-topology while also explicitly retaining inherited atrophy rules. The implementation's historical mode ID is `fixed_memory`, but growth and pruning remain enabled. It is a preallocation control, not a permanently fixed topology. This wording issue was identified after inspecting outcomes and is disclosed rather than repaired by changing the control or retuning growth. A definition is embedded in the summary. No result is presented as a cost-matched fixed-topology comparison.

## Interpretation

MORPH-002 supports order-sensitive structural timing at a chosen endpoint and timing-dependent information acquisition inside this authored developmental system. It also demonstrates an important null: structural endpoint divergence in the pruning-only case reconverges under shared continuation without a recall advantage. The stronger retained-memory finding is path dependence of acquired content, not lasting divergence in cognitive architecture.

The task schedule deliberately puts records between the two birth times. The evaluator authors both pressure and source truth, all recall is exact-key, and there is one deterministic history pair per case. This is expected mechanism behavior under controlled conditions, not evidence that development outperforms permanent memory, generalizes to independent tasks, creates personality, or establishes Article 28 support. The preallocated retained-memory control actually avoids the late-development acquisition deficit.

The natural next research gate is an independently authored workload with multiple resource-matched candidate structures and a genuinely pinned architecture control. That would test when conditional growth is advantageous rather than merely costly to delay. This report preserves the favorable positive result and the reconvergence result together.
